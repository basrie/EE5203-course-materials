Term Project Guidelines

EE5203 Microprocessors — 2026-2027 Fall

Author

Basri Erdogan

1 What You Will Build

In a team of 2 or 3 students, you will build an embedded system on the ST Nucleo-F401RE board. The system reads data from at least one sensor, processes it, shows or uses the result, and communicates over a serial bus. You develop it step by step through milestones that are checked in your lab sessions, and you finish with a demonstration and an individual oral defence. The project is 20% of your course grade.

3 Rules for Teams and Topics

  • A team has 2 or 3 members. Members may come from different lab sections; the team names one home section when it registers.
  • Choose a topic from Section 9 or propose your own. Your proposal in week 6 must be approved before you continue.
  • No two teams in the course can build the same project. The first team to register a topic gets it.
  • After the proposal you cannot change your team or topic without the instructor’s approval.
  • Parts: the course kit has no sensors. Your team buys the sensors, displays and actuators your project needs. Order them before the proposal; parts that arrive late do not move a milestone. Until a part arrives, you may use the potentiometer as a stand-in if you say so.

4 Technical Requirements

Your system must have all of these parts:

  1. Board and tools: Nucleo-F401RE, programmed in C with STM32CubeMX and VS Code (HAL or register level).
  2. Data acquisition: at least one sensor, read through the ADC or a digital interface.
  3. Processing: a clear method that turns raw data into a decision or a result (for example averaging and thresholds, a state machine, a controller).
  4. Output: show or use the result on a suitable device (display, LEDs, buzzer, servo or motor).
  5. Communication: at least one serial protocol — UART, I2C or SPI — between the board and a sensor, a display or a PC.

Minimum complexity. A sensor connected to one LED is not enough. Your system must use at least three of the course topics below, and its timing must not depend on HAL_Delay() in the main loop:

Course topic Lecture
External interrupts (EXTI) L06
Timers and non-blocking timing L07
PWM, output compare or input capture L08
ADC L09
UART L10
I2C or SPI L11

5 What You Submit

Upload one ZIP file per team to CATS by 29 November, 23:59. Name it EE5203_Project_TeamNN.zip. It contains:

  1. Source code: the CubeMX project (.ioc file and the Core folder). Do not include the build folder.
  2. Report (PDF, 10-15 pages) using the report template on CATS. It must contain:
    • the requirements table: each requirement, how you test it, and the measured result;
    • the resource map: every pin, timer, interrupt and peripheral you use, and what it does;
    • the timing budget: how often each task runs and how long it takes;
    • design decisions, wiring diagram, evidence (photos, logs, measurements) and known issues;
    • work distribution: which member wrote which part;
    • AI use: which tools you used and for what (see Section 7). Write “none” if you used none.
  3. Slides (PDF): 6 to 8 slides per team, not counting the title slide. Every member presents at least 2 of them.

Keep a working copy of your final code that you can flash in under two minutes on the day.

6 Demonstration and Oral Defence

All teams demonstrate in one joint session on Tuesday 1 December, from 13:00, in our laboratory room. The running order is announced on CATS. Every member must be present for the whole session.

15 minutes per team in total, presentation, demonstration and questions included. Set up while the team before you presents: your system must already be running when your slot starts. A slot that runs over is stopped.

Time What you do
0-8 min Presentation with live demonstration: every member presents at least 2 slides; show each requirement on the running system as you present it (one requirement, one test, one observed result)
8-9 min One fault you inject on purpose (say first what you expect)
9-15 min Individual questions: about 3 minutes per member in a team of two, 2 minutes in a team of three

Your known issues belong in the report; you may be asked about them.

Individual questions. Each member answers short questions about their own contribution, at up to three levels:

  1. Your contribution: “Which parts did you write? Walk me through one.”
  2. The mechanism: “Which register does that configure? What does the hardware do with it?”
  3. The alternative: “Why this way? What would happen if you did it differently?”

Be ready to open the code and the register view and point to the answer.

7 Use of AI Tools

These rules come from the course syllabus.

  • Allowed: asking an AI tool to explain a concept, a compiler or debugger message, or a datasheet passage; help with report wording; review of code you wrote yourself.
  • Not allowed: generating project code; producing the analysis or conclusions of the report with AI.
  • Disclosure: state in the report how you used AI tools.
  • Check: the oral defence. Work you cannot explain and change yourself earns no credit.

8 Grading

Part Points Graded per
Demonstration against your requirements 35 team
Report 25 team
Presentation (the slides you present) 15 member
Individual oral defence 25 member

Milestones. Each milestone in weeks 6, 9, 10 and 11 that is missed costs the team 5 points. A milestone is missed when the upload is not on CATS when the lab of your home section starts, or when the check in that lab does not pass.

Team points = demonstration + report (at most 60). Your share of the team points depends on your defence:

Your project grade = (team points − milestone deductions) × defence multiplier + your presentation points + your defence points

Your result in the oral defence Defence multiplier
You explain your contribution and the hardware mechanism behind it × 1
You explain your contribution, but not the mechanism (register, peripheral) × 0.5
You cannot explain your own contribution × 0 (project grade = 0)

Example. A team scores 30 (demonstration) + 20 (report) = 50 team points and missed no milestone.

Member Presentation and defence Calculation Project grade
A presentation 12; explains contribution and mechanism, defence 22 50 × 1 + 12 + 22 84
B presentation 10; explains contribution, not the mechanism, defence 12 50 × 0.5 + 10 + 12 47
C cannot explain own contribution 0 0

Electrical rules. The pins work at 3.3 V: a 5 V sensor output needs a voltage divider or a level shifter. Motors, pumps and fans need a driver with a flyback diode and their own supply, never a pin. No mains (230 V) wiring. The pulse-rate monitor is a demonstration, not a medical device.
