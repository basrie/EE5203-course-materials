  2. A synchronous transfer can pause mid-byte for a second and then resume correctly; a UART frame cannot. Explain why, in terms of how each receiver decides where a bit is.

  1. A student passes 0x68 straight to HAL. Which device do they actually address, and what will the call return?

  2. Explain ACK and NACK. Give one situation where the master sends a NACK deliberately, and say why.

  3. Why can two devices pulling SDA at the same time not damage each other? Name the property responsible.

  4. A team uses 10 kΩ pull-ups on a long bus and the signals look like slow ramps rather than square edges. Explain the cause and give the trade-off in choosing a smaller resistor.

Part C - I2C timing

  1. Compute the time for one byte plus ACK at 100 kHz and at 400 kHz. Show the bit count you used.

  2. The LCD backpack needs four I2C data bytes per character. Compute the time for one character and for a 16-character line, at both speeds.

  3. Your project has a 1 ms tick. Choose a display refresh rate and defend it with numbers, stating what fraction of available time the display consumes at your chosen rate.

  4. A teammate proposes refreshing the display every tick “because it is only a few bytes.” Evaluate this with numbers and give the resulting CPU load.

  1. A slave’s timing diagram shows the clock idling low, with data sampled on the falling edge. Give CPOL, CPHA, and the mode number.

  3. Your SPI reads return 0xFF constantly. Give three distinct causes, and explain why the peripheral reports no error for any of them.
